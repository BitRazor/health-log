What the last six minutes show

Legs raised is the opposite of standing

Raising the legs drains the lower-body veins toward the chest, so more blood returns to the heart with each beat. The heart moves more per stroke and needs fewer strokes — which is why the pulse settled at 68–71 bpm, the lowest reading of the whole session, only six minutes after the stretches.

Saturation was also at its best here: a 97.1% mean, the highest of the three phases, touching 98%.

Why a seated hour is worth noting

A stationary bike carries no postural load. The whole hour of effort happened without the gravity component that drives the standing pulse rise recorded on 11 August, so these numbers are a clean exertion response with posture removed.

That also means this session says nothing about the orthostatic picture in either direction — it measures a different axis.

On the protocol. An hour held between 120 and 160 bpm is vigorous, and the seated modality does not change that. The 13 August running note designates Sunday 16 August as light walking only inside the fasting scoring window, with training resuming Tuesday 18 August. Worth annotating in the fast's log so the endpoint is not read as confounded silently.

On the stop. The pre-registered prediction — effort is low risk, the stop is the risk window — survives this session untouched. Pulse came down as a taper rather than a cliff, and the minutes after the effort carried the highest saturations of the recording.

On capture. The app's note field never reaches the CSV export, which carries four columns only: time, oxygen level, pulse rate, motion. Activity context has to be captured separately or it is lost to any later analysis.

Not a diagnosis. A single home recording from a consumer fingertip oximeter, subject to a ±2% device accuracy. It is a data point for a clinician, not a clinical conclusion.
