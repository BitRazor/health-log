Why this does not look like sleep apnea

The heart rate is the tell

Obstructive events end in an arousal, and arousals drive the pulse up and down in a repeating cycle that overnight oximetry captures clearly. Nothing like that is present: minute-to-minute pulse varied by 2.4 bpm across the night, and moved 8 bpm or more on only 11 of 433 minutes.

Only 14 of 59 dips carried a pulse rise of 10 bpm or more.

The dips are scattered, not periodic

Apnea produces tightly packed runs, roughly one event per minute for minutes on end. Here the median gap between events is 2.8 minutes and the pattern is irregular.

Of the ten dips reaching 90% or below, six had a movement spike within a minute. Both 87% readings were immediately preceded by a pulse jump — to 85 and to 80 bpm against a 53 bpm baseline — while saturation was still 96–97%. A probe being disturbed does exactly that; a breathing event does not.

A whole night asleep produced roughly one seventieth the desaturation per hour of that awake seated evening. Whatever drove the 16 August event, sleep is not when it happens — which points further toward the awake, postural and breathing-pattern explanation and away from a sleep-breathing cause spilling into the day.

What this cannot do. Overnight pulse oximetry is a screen, not a sleep study. It sees saturation and pulse only — no airflow, no respiratory effort, no EEG. It therefore cannot separate central from obstructive events, and it cannot detect apneas that end in arousal without a measurable desaturation. The open question from the 2023 sleep study stays open; this recording narrows it rather than closing it.

One night only. Sleep-disordered breathing varies night to night with position, alcohol, congestion and sleep stage. A single clean night lowers the probability of a significant problem; it does not exclude one.

Not a diagnosis. Consumer fingertip oximeter, ±2% stated accuracy, and the open question of whether this unit reads a point or two low still stands. A one-off side-by-side against a clinical oximeter would settle it.
