The finding

What could produce this

The shape points at ventilation

A slow glide down over nine minutes, a flat plateau, then recovery within thirty seconds is the signature of reduced breathing rather than a circulation or sensor problem. Oxygen stores deplete gradually when tidal volume drops, settle at a new equilibrium, and refill almost immediately once normal breathing resumes.

Pulse stayed at 74–95 throughout — no compensatory tachycardia, which fits a mild, slowly-developing change rather than an acute event.

Three ordinary contributors

Absorbed shallow breathing. Breathing reliably gets shallower and more irregular during concentrated screen use. Gaming is a strong version of that state.

Slouched posture. A hunched seated position reduces functional lung volume and worsens the match between airflow and blood flow in the lung bases.

A recent meal. A full stomach raises the diaphragm and reduces lung volume further. Eating had just happened.

Those three together plausibly explain a drift to 88–89%. That said, a sustained 12-minute desaturation while awake and at rest is outside the normal range, and it is not something to file away on the strength of a plausible story alone.

What would settle it

  1. Repeat the conditions deliberately. Another gaming session with the oximeter on, sitting the same way. If the same drift appears, it is reproducible and real; if it never returns, this was a one-off.
  2. Test the posture hypothesis directly. When saturation is next seen below 91%, sit up straight and take ten slow deep breaths. Recovery within a minute confirms the ventilation mechanism and makes it self-correcting.
  3. Cross-check the device once. Wear the Checkme alongside a clinical oximeter at any appointment. That settles both this event and the standing question of whether the unit reads a point or two low.
  4. Bring it to the clinician. Repeat oximetry already sits on the never-done list, and the 2023 sleep study's central-versus-obstructive split is still unresolved. A daytime desaturation while awake is directly relevant to both.

Not a diagnosis. A single home recording from a consumer fingertip oximeter with ±2% stated accuracy. The controls above rule out movement and a systematically mis-reading sensor; they cannot rule out every probe-related cause, and they cannot establish what the desaturation means clinically.

The pulse side. From 20:22 to about 21:05 the heart rate ran 100–120 bpm while seated, roughly two hours after the bike session. Small hand movements only. Arousal during gaming is the ordinary explanation, and it is noted here because it overlaps the second half of the event window.
